WHY IT MATTERS

What Happens When We Mix Everything Up?

When food waste and wet garbage touch clean paper, the whole batch rots and ends up buried in landfills. Here is the difference proper sorting makes in your neighborhood.

🚫

The Mixed Garbage Problem

Throwing everything in one black bag
  • ✕
    Ruins Clean Recyclables

    Leftover curry, soup, or milk soaks into paper and cardboard, making them impossible to recycle.

  • ✕
    Dangerous Landfill Fires

    Old batteries get crushed inside waste trucks and easily catch fire, releasing toxic smoke.

  • ✕
    Foul Smells & Disease

    Unsorted piles rot rapidly in the open, attracting mosquitoes, stray animals, and pests.

✨

Clean Smart Segregation

Keeping wet, dry, and batteries separate
  • ✓
    Free Organic Plant Compost

    Kitchen food peels break down naturally into nutrient-rich black soil for local farms and parks.

  • ✓
    New Products from Old Items

    Clean plastic bottles become fleece jackets, and aluminum cans return to supermarket shelves in 60 days.

  • ✓
    Safer for Sanitation Workers

    Sanitation workers are protected from broken glass, toxic battery acid, and infectious hazards.

EASY REFERENCE

The Complete 4-Color Bin Guide

Keep this handy guide on your phone or fridge so your entire household sorts effortlessly.

GREEN BIN Wet / Organic Waste

All biodegradable kitchen and garden matter. Feeds natural microbes to create chemical-free fertilizer.

✅ PUT IN:
  • Vegetable peels & fruit skins
  • Leftover rice, bread, pasta
  • Coffee grounds & eggshells
  • Garden leaves & small twigs
❌ DO NOT PUT:
  • Plastic bags or wrappers
  • Pet waste or cat litter
  • Coated paper cups
  • Cigarette butts
BLUE BIN Dry Recyclable Waste

Clean, non-food-stained packaging. Must be kept dry so fibers and plastics can be washed and pelletized.

✅ PUT IN:
  • Clean cardboard boxes
  • Rinsed plastic water bottles
  • Soda & soup cans (aluminum/tin)
  • Glass jars & juice bottles
❌ DO NOT PUT:
  • Oily takeaway pizza boxes
  • Broken drinking glasses / mirrors
  • Potato chip / foil snack packets
  • Soiled napkins or tissues
AMBER / RED BIN E-Waste & Dangerous Items

Items with batteries, circuit boards, mercury, or chemicals. Highly dangerous if compacted in regular trucks!

✅ PUT IN:
  • Old AA / AAA & rechargeable batteries
  • Dead phones, laptops, cables
  • Fluorescent tubes & CFL bulbs
  • Expired medicines & paint cans
❌ DO NOT PUT:
  • Regular garbage or food scraps
  • Pressurized full gas cylinders
  • Wet kitchen towels
  • Standard paper or cardboard
GRAY / BLACK BIN General / Residual Waste

Items that cannot be recycled or composted safely. Sent for clean high-temperature energy recovery.

✅ PUT IN:
  • Dirty napkins & paper towels
  • Greasy pizza box bases
  • Multi-layer snack & candy wrappers
  • Sanitary & hygiene items (wrapped)
❌ DO NOT PUT:
  • Batteries or electronics (Fire risk!)
  • Clean recyclable paper or bottles
  • Fresh organic vegetable scraps
  • Construction bricks or timber
HOW MODERN CITIES PROCESS IT

From Your Bin to Next-Gen Sorting Hubs

Even when you sort at home, modern recycling facilities use smart sensors to double-check every item.

1

Optical Camera Vision

Smart cameras look at items moving on the belt to instantly tell apart paper, aluminum, and plastics.

2

Infrared Sensors

Invisible light beams check the exact plastic type (like milk jugs vs soda bottles) in 15 milliseconds.

3

Gentle Air-Jet Blowers

Targeted puffs of compressed air pop the right items into their own bins automatically at high speed.

4

Clean Repurposing

Pure materials are melted or pulped to create recycled goods without using virgin raw resources.
